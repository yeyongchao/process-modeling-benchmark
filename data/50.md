### Problem: Optimal Control of a Single-Tank Liquid Level System

## Input

A tank of uniform cross-sectional area $A$ has an outlet flow resistance $R$. The outlet volumetric flow rate is linearly related to the liquid head. Determine an optimization model over the time horizon $t \in [0,T]$ that regulates the liquid level by manipulating the inlet flow
$q(t)$.

![alt text](figures/50_psa_example_5_1_1.png)


## Output

### 1. Variables

- $q(t)$: inlet flow rate and manipulated control variable.
- $h(t)$: liquid level and state variable.
- $h_{\text{ref}}(t)$: prescribed reference liquid-level trajectory.
- $A$: uniform tank cross-sectional area (parameter).
- $R$: linear outlet-flow resistance (parameter).
- $h_0$: initial liquid level (parameter).
- $t \in [0,T]$: time, with $T$ the prescribed terminal time.

### 2. Objective Function

Choose $q(t)$ to minimize the liquid-level tracking error:

$$\min_{q(t)} J = \int_0^T \left( h(t) - h_{\text{ref}}(t) \right)^2 dt$$

### 3. Constraints

The dynamic balance is

$$A \frac{dh(t)}{dt} = q(t) - \frac{h(t)}{R}$$

with initial condition

$$h(0) = h_0$$

The outlet flow rate $q_o(t)$ is related linearly to the liquid head:

$$q_o(t) = \frac{h(t)}{R}$$

The physical nonnegativity conditions are

$$h(t) \ge 0, \qquad q(t) \ge 0, \qquad t \in [0,T].$$
