# Example 5.5: ILP Model for Scheduling the Operation of Thermal Power Units (the Unit Commitment Problem)


## Input
Decide when to start up and when to shut down a set of power plants to satisfy a varying demand over a time horizon while minimizing total operating costs.

Thermal power units are complex production units with significant costs associated with starting up or shutting down. There are also important constraints related to the rate at which power production can be increased or decreased while the plant is in operation. 

The problem evaluates the optimal scheduling of a set of power plants $i = \{1, 2, ..., N\}$ to meet a varying demand $D_t$ at each time period $t$ within a time horizon of interest $t = \{1, 2, ..., H\}$ (e.g., one day divided into 1-hour periods). 

Any thermal power unit $i$ can be characterized by its physical and economic parameters, including fixed costs, variable costs, startup/shutdown costs, min/max power output limits, and ramp-up/ramp-down limits. The state of each power unit at any time interval can be:
1. In normal operation
2. In shutdown state (not in operation)
3. In starting up state
4. In shutting down state

All events are assumed to take place at the beginning of each time interval and remain at the same state until the beginning of the next time interval.

## Output

### 1. Variables

* **Decision Variables (Binary):**
  * $y_{i,t} \in \{0, 1\}$: 1 if plant $i$ is in normal operation at time interval $t$, 0 otherwise
  * $u_{i,t} \in \{0, 1\}$: 1 if plant $i$ starts up at time interval $t$, 0 otherwise
  * $d_{i,t} \in \{0, 1\}$: 1 if plant $i$ shuts down at time interval $t$, 0 otherwise
* **Decision Variables (Continuous):**
  * $P_{i,t}$: Output power of unit $i$ at time $t$ (in MW)
* **Parameters:**
  * $C_{up,i}$: Startup cost of unit $i$ (\$)
  * $C_{dn,i}$: Shutdown cost of unit $i$ (\$)
  * $C_{fx,i}$: Fixed cost of operation of unit $i$ (\$/h)
  * $C_{vr,i}$: Unit variable cost of operation of unit $i$ (\$/h/MW)
  * $P_{M,i}$: Maximum output power of unit $i$ (MW)
  * $P_{m,i}$: Minimum output power of unit $i$ (MW)
  * $R_{up,i}$: Maximum power increment or ramp-up limit of unit $i$ (MW/h)
  * $R_{dw,i}$: Maximum power decrement or ramp-down limit of unit $i$ (MW/h)
  * $D_t$: Power demand at time $t$ (MW)

### 2. Objective Function

The objective is to minimize the total operating cost across all units and time periods, which includes fixed costs, variable costs, and penalties for startup and shutdown events:
$$\min_{y_{i,t}, u_{i,t}, d_{i,t}, P_{i,t}} \sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{t=1}^{H}(C_{fx,i}y_{i,t} + C_{vr,i}P_{i,t} + C_{up,i}u_{i,t} + C_{dn,i}d_{i,t})$$

### 3. Constraints

* **Logical State Relation:**
  The operating state of a plant relates to its startup and shutdown events. A plant in normal operation at time $t$ must have started up at some point and not shut down. This connects the binary variables (with initial states $y_{i,0}$ known):
  $$y_{i,t} - y_{i,t-1} = u_{i,t} - d_{i,t}, \quad \forall i,t$$

* **Power Output Limits:**
  If plant $i$ is in normal operation, its power output must be bounded by its minimum and maximum capacities. If the plant is not in operation ($y_{i,t}=0$), the output must be strictly 0:
  $$P_{m,i}y_{i,t} \le P_{i,t} \le P_{M,i}y_{i,t}, \quad \forall i,t$$

* **Demand Satisfaction:**
  The sum of the power output from all available operating units must satisfy the total varying power demand at each time interval:
  $$\sum_{i=1}^{N}P_{i,t} \ge D_t, \quad \forall t$$

* **Ramp-Up and Ramp-Down Limits:**
  When a unit is in normal operation in two consecutive intervals, its increase or decrease in output is restricted by the physical ramp-up and ramp-down limits (with initial state $y_{i,0}$ and initial power $P_{i,0}$ known):
  $$y_{i,t-1}=y_{i,t}=1 \implies -R_{dw,i} \le P_{i,t} - P_{i,t-1} \le R_{up,i}, \quad \forall i,t$$
  This constraint does not impose a ramp limit during startup or shutdown; no separate transition-ramp limits are specified.
